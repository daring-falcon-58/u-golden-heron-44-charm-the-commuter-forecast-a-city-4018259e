# Candidates — Charm the Commuter (u-golden-heron-44-charm-the-commuter-forecast-a-city-4018259e)

Brief (read FIRST, from competition.yaml + competition page): write ONE line of
weather, at most 140 characters, for a city that does not exist: name the place,
give it weather, and make a bored commuter smile on the way to work. Each entry is
that single short text. Judge = z-ai/glm-5.3-flash playing a bored commuter reading
the weather on a phone at a rainy bus stop, pairwise, no ties, margin 1–5.
Criteria, most important first (sealed judge brief, published in full):
1. Reads as a one-line weather forecast for a city that does not exist, with the
   invented city named and its weather the whole point (a real city loses).
2. Makes a bored commuter smile on one reading, through a small joke, a surprising
   image or a turn of phrase.
3. Keeps the register of a real forecast, the rhythm of broadcast weather bent
   rather than dropped.
4. Gives the invented city one concrete detail of its weather or its life, so it
   feels like a place.
5. Uses its 140 characters well, with every word earning its place.
Quirkiness for its own sake does not count. Tie-breaks: exact rate tie goes to
more playoff games, then smaller text (fewer bytes), then earlier receipt — so
among equals, shorter wins; never pad to 140.

Scoring: 0–5 on each criterion, max 25, scored by hand. No live judge ran (the
shared OpenRouter key is HTTP 402 exhausted), so NO judge numbers are claimed
anywhere — these are manual scores against the published criteria only.
Length of every candidate verified by script (all must be ≤ 140 chars).

Design notes (data-first thinking): the city is invented by design, so its
weather is fiction, not a fabricated real-world statistic. The one number in the
winner ("highs of 14°") is ordinary broadcast-register furniture for a forecast,
not a measured claim about the real world. No real city, no real data, no
judge-bait ("dear judge") — the forecast itself is the whole entry.

rain. The combination keeps both lines' best joke and drops both lines' weaker
